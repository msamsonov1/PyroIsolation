Mathematical formulations for high-current DC interruption in vacuum-tight lunar habitats

In high-voltage DC distribution (1000V DC lunar microgrids), zero-current crossings do not exist naturally. Interruption requires mechanical gap generation combined with forced energy extraction where arc dissipation exceeds electrical energy input ($P_{\text{arc}} > V_{\text{bus}} I$). The PyroIsolation system models this phase transition in real-time.

1. Modified Cassie-Mayr Dynamic Arc Model

The arc conductance $G(t)$ in a high-velocity mechanical shearing gap follows a combined Cassie-Mayr energy dissipation equation governed by thermodynamic cooling time constants $\theta$ and arc loss power $P_0$:

$$\frac{1}{G}\frac{dG}{dt} = \frac{1}{\theta(v_p, P_{\text{puff}})} \left( \frac{u_{\text{arc}} \cdot i}{P_0(t)} - 1 \right)$$

As the chemical combustion energy $E_{\text{pyro}}$ propels the cutter blade at velocity $v_p(t)$, the mechanical separation $x(t) = \int v_p dt$ creates a rapidly lengthening column. If the local gas thermal conduction and convection cannot overcome the joule heating $u_{\text{arc}} \cdot i$, then $\frac{dG}{dt} \to 0$ or becomes positive, triggering flashover.

2. Piston Kinematics & Chemical Energy Partition

The pyrotechnic charge burns rapidly, converting chemical explosive energy $E_{\text{chem}}$ into high-pressure propellant gases that drive the hardened ceramic cutter piston:

$$E_{\text{total}} = \int_0^t F_{\text{prop}} \, dx = \frac{1}{2} m_p v_p^2 + W_{\text{shear}} + E_{\text{plasma}} + \Delta E_{\text{thermal}}$$

Where $m_p$ is piston mass, $W_{\text{shear}} = \int \tau_u A_{\text{bus}} dx$ is work expended shearing the copper busbar, and $E_{\text{plasma}}$ is the trapped metallic vapor thermal ionization energy. Residual ionized gas creates plasma blow-by around mechanical sealing rings.

3. Real-Time $(E, G)$ Observer & Arrest Detection

Dual optical emission spectrometry (measuring Cu-I vapor line intensity at 521.8 nm) combined with Rogowski coil current derivative $\frac{di}{dt}$ calculates instantaneous arc energy $E(t)$ and conductance $G(t)$:

$$E(t) = \int_0^t \left( V_{\text{bus}} - L_{\text{bus}} \frac{di}{dt} \right) \cdot i(\tau) \, d\tau, \quad G(t) = \frac{i(t)}{u_{\text{arc}}(t)}$$

The software tracks the trajectory vector $\vec{\Gamma} = (E, G)$ in real-time. If $\left| \frac{dG}{dt} \right| < \epsilon_{\text{stall}}$ while $G > G_{\text{critical}}$, the controller detects an incomplete quench arrest and initiates auxiliary gas injection within $25\,\mu\text{s}$.

4. Supersonic Dielectric Injection Recovery

The auxiliary puff injects a high-dielectric mixture ($SF_6 / N_2$ or compressed dry Fluoroketone) directly into the shearing knife gap, drastically raising the dielectric breakdown voltage $V_{\text{bd}}$ via Paschen's generalized law:

$$V_{\text{bd}} = \frac{B \cdot P \cdot d}{\ln(A \cdot P \cdot d) - \ln\left[ \ln\left(1 + \frac{1}{\gamma_{\text{se}}}\right) \right]}$$

The turbulent supersonic gas flush sweeps out metal vapor charge carriers, collapsing the arc time constant $\theta \to 0.4\,\mu\text{s}$ and permanently driving conductance $G \to 0\,\text{S}$ before thermal restrike occurs.
